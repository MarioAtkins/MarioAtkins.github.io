ENTRY 02 / 103 · YEAR 1987.4

Elmer is born

Pressure score: 20

People / context: Elmer, Mom, Father

My brother Elmer was born on May 25, 1987. I remember being excited to have a little brother. Looking back, I also recognize this was around when the atmosphere inside our home began changing. Stress, drinking, arguments, and anger became more common. As a child I didn't understand what I was watching. As an adult, I can see how difficult parenthood, financial pressure, and emotional immaturity affected both of my parents.

ENTRY 03 / 103 · YEAR 1987.7

Learning fear at home

Pressure score: 82

People / context: Father, Mom

One memory has never left me. I was told to stand in the bathroom as punishment and not move. I needed to use the toilet but was too afraid to move because I believed I would be beaten if I disobeyed. I urinated on myself instead. I remember my father telling my mother there was something wrong with that boy. Looking back, there wasn't anything wrong with me. I was terrified. That memory has become one of the clearest examples of how fear shaped my behavior as a child.

ENTRY 04 / 103 · YEAR 1988.0

My father leaves

Pressure score: 88

People / context: Father, Mom

Around 1988 my father left our household. One of my last memories of him before he disappeared was watching him take a Polaroid photograph of my mother from behind while she wore a blue jean overall dress. I carried that photograph with me for years. As I grew older I learned more about what had been happening in their relationship, including him wanting my mother to have an abortion while pregnant with Elmer, and disturbing stories about him talking about harming me as an infant. I believe those moments may have been where my mother drew the line. She always corrected me whenever I said he had left. He didn't leave, she'd say, we separated. Years later, drugs and circumstances in Louisiana left him unable to speak, confined to a wheelchair, for the remainder of his life.

Before Chicago became home, Louisiana was my earliest world. I remember church every Sunday, sometimes returning for evening service and again on Wednesdays. I remember my father carrying me into church. I remember cotton candy. Those memories are among the few peaceful ones I have with him.

ENTRY 06 / 103 · YEAR 1988.1

Aunt Michelle

Pressure score: 15

People / context: Aunt Michelle

One of my happiest childhood memories is my Aunt Michelle. She drove a beautiful light-blue convertible that completely fascinated me as a child. One evening she picked us up and drove us home. Looking back now, that memory makes me smile more than anything else because it reminds me childhood wasn't only trauma. There were moments of kindness, safety, and people who genuinely cared for me.

ENTRY 23 / 103 · YEAR 1997.3

ENTRY 25 / 103 · YEAR 1999

Century Lane

Pressure score: 75

People / context: Mom, Elmer, Phillip, cousins

This is when we left Montgomery and moved to Century Lane, into Section 8 housing on the corner, the same block where my cousins Phillip and them already lived. I was so excited about the move that I rode my own bike there myself, following the truck. It was miles away, maybe ten, past Waldo Middle School, past New York Street, up to Ohio Street to get there. I'd bought that bike, a Dino with the gold flip-down pegs, off a friend for sixty dollars, using money I'd taken from my mom's purse, something I felt ashamed about later. I loved that bike enough to way overpay for it and pedal it the whole distance myself instead of riding in the truck. It got stolen less than two months after we moved in, one more thing lost in the hood that you just had to get over because you'd never know who took it. My mother's mental health cracking open in real time: talking to herself, screaming at God, believing someone was banging when Elmer and I were banging on walls. I took money from her purse, felt guilt, used imagination as escape, and learned to survive inside instability.

ENTRY 29 / 103 · YEAR 2001.6

Riding for batteries

Pressure score: 86

People / context: Darnell, Aurora police ("Superman")

Age 16, July/August 2001. Around 11pm I rode my mountain bike to my older cousin Darnell's apartment to borrow batteries, since we had no money to buy any. The route crossed Ohio Street toward King Street, the edge of Latin King territory everyone knew to avoid. Turning onto King Street, headlights suddenly came on behind me and the car accelerated. My first thought wasn't police, it was that I was about to be killed. I pedaled harder than I ever had, abandoned the bike at Darnell's, and dove through a broken aluminum panel under his screened porch to hide, staying still while flashlights swept the yard. It turned out to be Aurora police, including an officer known in the neighborhood as "Superman" who later became known for excessive force complaints. They searched me at gunpoint and held me in a paddy wagon for twenty to thirty minutes trying to decide what to charge me with, eventually settling on curfew violation. Inside, Darnell and his girlfriend had been smoking, and she was furious that I'd supposedly led police to their door. From mine, I'd spent the last several minutes believing I was running for my life. Looking back, the batteries were never the story. The story was how normal it had become to assume an ordinary errand could end in gangs, police, or violence.

ENTRY 30 / 103 · YEAR 2001.75

Mark's last night

Pressure score: 95

People / context: Mark, Mr. Muhammad, Phillip

I'd left my Jay-Z Blueprint CD at Mark's house and almost went back to get it, since everyone wanted to hear Renegade. Instead I kept my word to Mr. Muhammad, who'd asked me to come over and watch an old movie about Moses. We stayed up talking until almost four in the morning, and I walked home at sunrise. A short time later Phillip was pounding on my door: Mark had been shot, the car shot up seventeen times, dead instantly. I'd seen him only six or seven hours earlier. The only thing that had kept me from being there was not wanting to break my word to Mr. Muhammad. For years afterward I couldn't shake the thought that I almost went back.

ENTRY 32 / 103 · YEAR 2002.0

First arrest

Pressure score: 70

People / context: Darion, Tommy, police

After an altercation with Darion, a friend of Elmer's, where he put hands on me and I slapped him hard in front of a crowd, he went home to Ohio Street and told his uncle Tommy, a much bigger, volatile man. A few days later Tommy waited hidden near Maxine's house, ambushed me from behind while I was walking home shirtless, and beat me down before I even knew what was happening. ENTRY 33 / 103 · YEAR 2002.1

Juvenile detention for truancy

Pressure score: 82

People / context: Public defender, judge

I was sentenced to spend nearly my entire summer in juvenile detention for truancy. My public defender, a young white attorney, warned me beforehand that the judge had a reputation for making examples out of minority kids in his courtroom. He was right. The judge scheduled it so it consumed my whole summer. It was one of the worst experiences of my adolescence: bad food, rigid schedule, boys separated from girls, occasional visits from a preacher, kids who seemed disturbingly at home there. I cried one night, feeling like I was losing time I'd never get back. The record was later expunged. Shortly after release I found work at the Gap.

ENTRY 50 / 103 · YEAR 2006.92

Jerry, Marshall, and the iron

Candice was staying with me in the trailer one night when Marshall, who I later learned was using cocaine, got agitated and confronted us holding an iron. ENTRY 51 / 103 · YEAR 2006.94
