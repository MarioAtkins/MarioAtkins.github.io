If you have another minute · A little about me

Good design gives people fewer things to work around.

I've spent most of my career noticing the things that slow people down. A confusing process, a missing explanation, or a workaround everybody accepts because it's always been there.

After joining Chime, I went through onboarding, identity verification, mobile check deposit, and support. So far, the steps have felt thoughtfully connected. I haven't had to learn a trick to make something work. After nearly 15 years in financial services, I appreciate how much thinking goes into that kind of simplicity.

Relevant experience

I already know the work your learners do.

My route into learning design has been through the operations themselves. I've coached the people doing the work, studied what customers say about it, and tried to make complicated steps easier to understand.

01 / ANALYZE

Find the real problem.

At Space Coast Credit Union, I used Qualtrics feedback and Excel exports to categorize complaints and investigate recurring patterns. I separated knowledge gaps from workflow and policy problems before recommending a response.

Feedback → root cause → action
02 / EXPLAIN

Make complexity legible.

I designed a visual wire-transfer process in Visio and participated in cross-functional work to make online wire instructions clearer. I like translating a process into something people can examine and discuss.

Operations → visual explanation
03 / DEVELOP

Give learning a job.

I mentored employees, built knowledge quizzes and JeopardyLabs activities, and created procedural job aids. More recently, I've directed AI-assisted workflow and documentation projects, checking outputs against source material and actual system behavior.

Learning → application → review
How I think

Before the lesson, ask better questions.

When an agent makes the same mistake repeatedly, I'd want to examine the evidence before deciding that another course will fix it.

Is this a knowledge gap, or is the process itself getting in the way?

That question guides the work I enjoy. It's also why Chime's Disputes Experience learning role feels so closely connected to my background.
