Participation Matters

Participant, signer, CC contact, and witness are different roles

Counsel / Transaction Participant

If the Proof transaction supports additional participants, counsel or another authorized transaction contact may join the live meeting. Participation alone does not make that person a document witness.

Signer

If counsel is actually required to sign the document, counsel should be configured and treated as a signer for that transaction.

Document Witness

A witness role is used only when the document or applicable law requires witnessing. Witnessing is separate from simply attending the meeting and may require the witness to sign.

CC / Completed-Document Recipient

When the transaction setup supports it and the sender authorizes it, an attorney or firm contact can be designated to receive the completed document. That routing role does not by itself determine statutory rights to the RON journal or recording.

Proof states that a meeting can support up to 10 connected devices, with one notary and one signer required and remaining slots available for other participants, subject to transaction configuration. Witness roles have separate rules and limits.

Before the First Client

Map the workflow once when the document repeats

For repeat estate-planning or legal documents, the cleanest relationship is to agree in writing on the operational pieces before the client enters the session: who supplies the document, who must sign, whether witnesses are required, where signatures and notarial fields belong, who receives the completed document, who handles legal questions, and when counsel should join.

If the document or instructions materially change, the workflow should be reviewed again rather than assuming an old setup still applies.

Records & Access

A durable record without overstating who is entitled to it

Florida RON requires an electronic journal and an uninterrupted audio-video recording of the online notarization, retained for at least 10 years.

Florida law gives specified people and entities rights to pertinent journal entries and/or the related recording. Being the referring attorney, sender, or CC contact does not automatically make someone a statutory “party to the electronic record.” Record access depends on the statute and the applicable platform permissions.
